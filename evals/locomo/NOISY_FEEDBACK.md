# LoCoMo: learned usefulness under noisy feedback

Generated by `python -m evals.locomo.report --noisy` from `noisy_results.json` (produced by `python -m evals.locomo.run --sweep`). Do not edit by hand.

The LoCoMo results in [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md) train usefulness on *oracle* feedback: for each training question the evidence turns among the top 10 are marked used and the other shown turns ignored. A real agent's feedback is what it cites, which is noisier and sparser. This sweep keeps everything else fixed (the same leave-one-conversation-out folds, stores, nested-CV tuning grid and 1982 test questions) and only replaces the training feedback with a simulated agent. The held-out conversation still gets no feedback.

## Feedback models

Applied per training question to the top 10 it recalled, starting from the oracle:

- `oracle`: evidence turns used, other shown turns ignored (the committed protocol).
- `miss=p`: each shown evidence turn is independently not cited with probability p (the agent forgot to cite it). By default a missed turn counts as *ignored*, because an agent that does not cite something signals ignore; `(unmarked)` rows leave it without feedback instead.
- `false_pos=q`: each shown non-evidence turn is independently marked used with probability q (the agent cited something irrelevant).
- `sparse=r`: only a share r of training questions give any feedback; the rest are recalled and left unmarked. `sparse=0` is *no feedback at all*: the learned methods keep their tuned weights but usefulness stays at the prior. It is the floor that separates the effect of learning from that of importance and tuning.
- `cite_top1`: only the highest-ranked shown evidence turn is marked used, other evidence turns are left unmarked, non-evidence turns are ignored (an agent that cites one source).
- Combined *realistic* setting: `miss=0.5,false_pos=0.1,sparse=0.5`.
- *Stress* points beyond the planned grid, added to find where the gain breaks: `miss=0.9`, `false_pos=0.4`, `sparse=0.02`, `miss=0.7,false_pos=0.2,sparse=0.25`. They are marked † in the tables.

Noise is deterministic: each draw is a hash of (seed, conversation, question, turn), so a question's feedback is the same in every fold and method, and noise levels are nested (with one seed, what is missed at `miss=0.3` is also missed at `miss=0.5`). Stochastic settings run with seeds [0, 1, 2]; deterministic ones (`oracle`, `cite_top1`, `sparse=0`) once.

**Statistics.** For a stochastic setting each question's reciprocal rank is first averaged over the seeds, then the difference to the baseline is bootstrapped over questions (paired, 10 000 resamples, the same resamples as RESULTS.md). The estimate is the expected ΔMRR under that noise model; the CI covers question sampling. Spread across seeds (noise realisations) is shown separately. With one seed this is exactly the RESULTS.md bootstrap. *Significant* = the 95% CI excludes 0. `bm25` and `bm25+embedding+tuned` do not read usefulness, so they are identical under every feedback model (checked).

## Reading

**Within the planned grid the gain of `heapdb+emb+learned+tuned` over `bm25+embedding+tuned` never stops being significant.** It shrinks with the noise, to +0.028 [+0.022, +0.033] at its lowest (`miss=0.7`). Of the stress points, none breaks it. The margin cannot fall much below the no-feedback margin (next paragraph), so the stricter question is whether learning itself still helps: its contribution is smallest at `miss=0.9` †, +0.003 [+0.000, +0.006] (lower bound at or near 0: learning has essentially stopped helping).

With oracle feedback `heapdb+emb+learned+tuned` − `bm25+embedding+tuned` is +0.051 [+0.043, +0.058]. ## Threats to validity

- **Simulated, not real, agent feedback.** Errors are independent per turn and uniform: a real agent's misses and false citations depend on content and rank. In particular, real false citations tend to be plausible distractors (lexically close to the question), which would bias the learned features more than uniform noise does. No LLM judge was run.
- **The noise is fixed per question.** A training question gets the same feedback in each of the 9 folds it trains in. That is how a logged agent would behave, but it means the 3 seeds are 3 noise realisations, not 27; the seed spread is in the *All methods* table.
- **Usefulness = evidence.** The oracle (and every model here) treats LoCoMo's annotated evidence turns as what an agent would use. An agent may legitimately use other turns.
- **Feedback only on the top 10**, one query per training question and no repeated recalls of the same memory: per-memory links never help on held-out data. In real use they would, and they would also absorb per-memory noise.
- **Tuning does not adapt to the noise.** Weights (including the usefulness weight, fixed at 1) are tuned without learning. A noise-aware tuner might down-weight usefulness when feedback is poor, which would make the learned rows more robust than shown here.
- **One benchmark, one embedder, small grids**, as in RESULTS.md.

## Setup and reproduction

- Dataset sha256 `79fa87e90f04081343b8c8debecb80a9a6842b76a7aa537dc9fdf651ea698ff4`; 1982 questions; ranking depth 50; feedback on the top 10.
- Embeddings: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 ONNX, sha256 `6fd5d72fe4589f189f8ebc006442dbb529bb7ce38f8082112682524616046452`.
- 57 runs; sum of per-run runtimes 11864.6 s (runs were split across parallel processes) (Python 3.13.16, x86_64).
- `python -m evals.locomo.run --onnx-model DIR --sweep` (resumable; `--shard i/n` splits it across processes, then `--sweep --collect`), then `python -m evals.locomo.report --noisy`.
